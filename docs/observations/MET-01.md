# MET-01: Hana donko shiitake by staged fan direction

**Evidence level:** E4, the grower's own method. It is unblinded and has no untreated comparison logs. No weights or counts are recorded, by design (the project does not measure yield).

## Method, as practised (corrected 2026-11-19)

A fan runs beside the fruiting shiitake logs. Its **direction changes with the stage of the flush**:

| Stage | Fan direction |
|---|---|
| Early (primordia to young buttons) | **Pointed away** from the fruit bodies |
| Mid (caps expanding, still closed) | **Pointed directly at the caps** |
| Late (caps near opening, to harvest) | **Pointed away** from the fruit bodies again |

Earlier write-ups said the opposite pattern, with indirect air from mid-stage and direct air when the caps neared gill opening. That was wrong. The grower's own description above replaces it.

Earlier notes also give the fan as 30–60 cm from the logs, with the aim of drying only the cap skin so that it cracks white. Those details have not been re-confirmed.

Because flushes come at irregular times, the grower manages the fan flush by flush. It is not on a fixed schedule.

## Repeats

| Flushes managed this way | Dates | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Autumn 2026, irregular (unscheduled) flushes; dates not recorded | Hana donko each time: closed caps with deep white cracking and inrolled margins |

The first hana donko on konara was reported on 2026-10-30 (OBS-04).

The grower reports hana donko on **3 of 3** flushes managed this way (reported 2026-11-19).

## What it does and does not show

* **It shows** that the method has repeatedly produced hana donko on the grower's konara logs.
* **It does not show** that the fan caused the cracking. Hana donko depends on weather as well (dry air while the caps grow), and there were no logs left without the fan in the same flush. Nor does it show which stage of fan exposure matters.
* **A clean test:** split the logs of one flush into fan and no-fan groups at random, and score each cap blind from photos on the project's grading scale (hana donko / donko / koshin). Record humidity with a logger. No weighing is needed.
