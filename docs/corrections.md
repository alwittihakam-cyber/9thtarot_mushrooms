# Corrections to earlier project documents

Building this dataset meant re-reading 11 sources against their originals on 2026-11-19. These are the points where the project's earlier reports (the Species Compendium, the Kaname River tree ranking and the Log Yard Plan v2) said more than the sources do, or something different. The dataset follows the sources.

| # | Earlier claim | What the source says | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reishi: "ume-sawdust mycelium most active … reishi from ume trees beat other broadleaf hosts", read as support for ume as the best reishi wood (plan grade A, 25 logs) | Kumakura et al. 2008 measured **bioactivity** (ACE, platelet and IL-8 assays). It reports no yield data. | Reishi on ume is now "hypothesis only" for yield. Proposed trial P05. |
| 2 | Kikurage: "Saga: akamegashiwa 32.3 kg/m³, top or second in every year" | Akamegashiwa was best in two batches (32.3 and 37.8) but gave only **4.1** in the 2014 batch, behind ミズキ 49.6 and イチイガシ 24.5. | Kept as excellent at E2, with a conflict flag. |
| 3 | Kikurage: "Saga 2013: willow 12.1 kg" | 12.1 is the **4-year cumulative** total. Year 1 alone was 5.2 in one table and 0.2 in the other, which covers a shorter harvest window. | Graded good, not excellent. |
| 4 | Kikurage trials stated without a species | Every Japanese log trial cited used **アラゲキクラゲ (*Auricularia cornea*)**. | Each row is flagged. Transfer to *A. heimuer* is untested. |
| 5 | Kikurage: "Aomori ranked alder > willow > konara" (willow read as native willow) | The willow was **マツダナヤナギ (*Salix matsudana*)**, an introduced fast-growing willow. The report gives ranks only, with no yields. | Kept as rank-only E2. |
| 6 | Lion's mane / shiitake: "ツブラジイ fruits one year only" (listed under lion's mane) and "アラカシ matched konara early for shiitake" | In Aichi, the one-year result was for **hiratake**. Aichi has **no shiitake data on アラカシ**. Its アラカシ result is for maitake. | Lion's mane and shiitake rows removed. Hiratake row added as marginal. |
| 7 | Hiratake: "Beech > poplar > chestnut, linden failed; ツブラジイ one year only", cited to Aichi | The beech, poplar, chestnut and linden ranking is **Sarıyer et al. 2023 (Turkey)**. The ツブラジイ result is Aichi. | Split into two sources. Sarıyer is the dataset's only verified E1 wood trial. |
| 8 | Nameko: "Sawdust of 20+ broadleaves … konara alone not optimal; クリ and ウワミズザクラ poor" | **16** species. It is a **sawdust-bed** trial, so it does not show how logs behave. Konara gave about the same yield as the control, just not among the best five. | Graded as sawdust-bed. Log claims take precedence in the matrix. |
| 9 | Ibaraki guide cited for hinoki working for nameko | The Ibaraki table has no hinoki entry. Hinoki for nameko comes from Cha et al. 2010, which is about nameko, as its title confirms, not hiratake as the tree ranking said. | Reattributed. |
| 10 | Maitake on sugi: "8 of 9 strains fruited" | Not found in the Aichi report text. It gives 77.9% and 87.8% of konara for two strains, and one strain gave nothing. | Removed. |
| 11 | Eringi not linked to any log trial | Aichi tried eringi on **6 log species**. **None fruited**, a failure attributed to contamination. | Added as E2 "none". This is useful context for the carrot method. |
| 12 | Hackberry treated as an elm relative for golden oyster | *Celtis* is now placed in **Cannabaceae** (APG IV), not Ulmaceae. Zelkova is the true elm-family tree. | Both stay E5. Zelkova is the stronger analogue. |
